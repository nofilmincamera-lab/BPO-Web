Provenance discipline

RFP §1.12 treats unstated assumptions as acceptance, so an animation that puts Klarna's internal wiring on screen is making claims under that rule whether it means to or not. Every mechanic below carries a tag, and the tag decides whether it can appear unqualified.

TagMeaningOn-screen rule
EEvidenced Published Klarna or RFP source — EMD field schemas, Identity API operations, the 13-queue list and SLAs, retained scope (§4.3), the QA model (§7.1), the Control Catalog. Show freely, including exact field names.
IInferred Engine-to-queue and inter-queue wiring; Klarna-internal, not published. Show only under the existing illustrative-model footer. Never as confirmed Klarna wiring.
PFoundever-proposed Our operating design, not a Klarna requirement — L1→L2 triggers, L2 outcome authorities, four-eye placement, SAR Option B. Must read as a proposal; frames carrying several get a second-line qualifier.
NNarrative From the audio, carrying no process claim. Never dressed as a system state.

Three hard prohibitions this creates

  • No invented risk score. Klarna publishes no thresholds. The threshold edge is labelled with the real edge condition — cannot auto-clear.
  • No invented engine names. The UK Privacy Notice establishes that there are five automated decision categories; the contents are not published. Cite the count, never the contents.
  • No itemised basket in the case file. Purchase-category EMD packages carry no case-evidence value. The analyst gets the flag and the merchant category — drawing a shopping list is a visible process error.

Two persistent additions

  • The automation river. Clean tokens stream through Stage 1 at 15% for all 118 s, behind everything. The film as storyboarded implies automation hands off and waits; it does not. Foundever's judgment layer is a side-channel on a river that never stops — and the river is the denominator the deal is priced on.
  • The retained column stands, it does not queue. From Shot 14 the four retained labels stay lit and untouched. Klarna's authority is a standing property of the model, not a station the case passes through.

Decisions before build

Five open calls. Recommendations are marked; D1 is a deliberate departure from a stated storyboard rule.

D1

Does the camera scale to the active stage through the middle, or hold wide for all 115 seconds?

Scale. The cognitive-load rule was written for 60 seconds; a fixed wide shot for 115 reads as static, and the 2.76 s pull-back at 102.72 s is wasted without it. Composition and stage order never change, which is what that rule actually protects.

D2

The outcome is an approval, not a fraud finding. Is false-positive cost the argument we want leading?

Yes — and say so in the deck. It is the stronger commercial argument for an evaluator who already owns the fraud-loss case. But note the film does not demonstrate a confirmed-suspicion path. If evaluators need that, it is a second asset, not an extension of this one.

D3

QA gets 2.00 s of silence and no narration. Accept, or record a VO pickup?

Accept. Three 300 ms checks fit with room. A pickup would break the read's rhythm and the ring composition. If QA must carry more weight for a specific evaluator, give it a dedicated PDF frame rather than more screen time.

D4

“Clears straight through” is stronger than the storyboard's permitted learning register.

Keep the audio; carry the qualification on screen. The governance label beside the return path is mandatory and must hold legible for the full 5.8 s of Shot 17. Do not re-record.

D5

Is the customer ribbon in scope?

Yes. One line of persistent furniture, and the only way “the customer never felt the review” lands as evidence rather than assertion. It has to be unbroken for ninety seconds before it can pay off in three.

D6

Does Klarna approve this case, or confirm the boundary while L2 closes within delegated authority?

Confirm the boundary. Shot 13 sets the disposition to Resolved: no report, which our own model closes within delegated authority. Animating an approval contradicts the model we are bidding, trips the storyboard's prohibitions on sending every case to Klarna and making Klarna a rework step, and argues for less autonomy than the commercial case needs. The narration supports the boundary reading exactly — “keeps what only Klarna should hold” describes a standing column, not a transaction. The most consequential open call in the film.

D7

Build the ops layer as a toggleable group, or leave the exec cut as the only asset?

Build both. One extra pass on the same renders. The exec cut wins the evaluation; the ops cut wins the room where someone stops the video and asks what the analyst was looking at.

D8

Name the queue on screen, or stay queue-agnostic?

Name it — Q13, Fraud 24/7 Real-Time Monitoring. It is evidenced, it makes the 21:40 timestamp and the real-time resolution mutually reinforcing, and it demonstrates we know which of the thirteen we are in.

D9

Show the market-anchor line — no registry key in euro markets?

Yes. One mono sub-label in Shot 2. It is the strongest available evidence that judgment is a structural requirement of Klarna's market footprint rather than a service we are selling into it.

D10

Show both QA gates, or the single gate as storyboarded?

Both. §7.1 specifies Klarna AI-first QC on every case. A single mint gate understates Klarna's own control and wastes a governance proof that costs 0.7 s.

Build order

Not chronological. Build the shots that carry the argument first, so a failure is found while it is still cheap.

  1. Shot 12 — the inversion. The film's central idea. If the chip reorder doesn't read, nothing downstream matters.
  2. Shot 14 — the boundary. The governance proof, and the shot an RFP evaluator will scrub back to.
  3. Shots 2 + 10. Record card and its strike-throughs — one component, two states. Build together.
  4. Shot 17. The learning loop, with the governance label locked in from the first pass.
  5. Shot 3 + persistent architecture. The three-chip component and all stationary furniture.
  6. Shots 1 + 19. The ring — one composition in two states, never two frames.
  7. The ops layer. Lane rail, field-name sub-labels, queue tags — one pass over the finished exec cut.
  8. Everything else.

Acceptance checks

Carried from the storyboard's production checklist. Mint boxes are the four this cut adds.

  • The proposition is legible within five seconds.
  • Stage order and composition never change; only scale and the case move.
  • Foundever is visibly absent from the clean automated path.
  • L1 and L2 have distinct purposes — L1 structures, L2 resolves sequence.
  • The authority boundary is a hard line, and the token does not cross it.
  • QA resolves before closure, not after.
  • The learning loop returns insight to Klarna-controlled automation, label legible throughout.
  • The second case completes without starting a new story.
  • Every beat has a clean, printable settled frame.
  • The three signals invert rather than being replaced — same chips, reordered and recoloured.
  • The decline column never resolves into a state — ghosted and dashed for its full life.
  • The customer ribbon is unbroken from 0:05 to 118:00, and brightens exactly once.
  • Shots 1 and 19 are the same composition in two states, not two different frames.
  • The automation river runs unbroken for all 118 s and is never idle.
  • Foundever's lane is visibly dark for the whole of Shot 2, not just at the routing moment.
  • No numeric risk score, and no itemised basket, appears anywhere.
  • The third signal chip reads as an engine conclusion, not a record fact.
  • Nothing ever queues on Klarna's side of the boundary.
  • Both QA gates appear, with their two owners visually distinct.
  • Model risk management · Tier D is legible for the full learning-loop shot.

Final comprehension test. Show frame 8 for five seconds, then hide it. The viewer should be able to say: “Klarna's automation flags what it should. Foundever resolves the judgment. Klarna keeps the decisions that are Klarna's. QA gates the close. And the next case is better informed.”
